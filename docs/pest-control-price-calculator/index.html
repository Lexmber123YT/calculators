How this works

First it finds what one visit costs you. That is the materials, plus every minute of tech time (on site and driving) at your cost per hour, plus the drive minutes at your truck cost per minute. Drive time counts twice on purpose: the tech is paid for it and the truck wears for it.

Then it finds the lowest price that keeps your profit target. The cost is divided by (1 minus your target), and rounded up to the next dollar. At a 30% target, a $46.50 visit needs at least $67.

Last, it checks the prices you charge now. Profit is your price minus the cost. A price whose profit is under your target share of that price is marked "Below target", and one under the cost is marked "Loses money". Profit per tech hour divides that profit by the on-site and drive time together. This answers what a visit earns and the price floor for your target. It does not set your market price: check what others near you charge, then make sure your price is above this floor.

What the usual free answers miss

  • Most guides give a national price range. Your floor depends on what your tech time, truck and materials cost, not on an average.
  • Drive time is the cost most often left out. A cheap monthly stop with a long drive can fall below target even when the price looks fine.
  • First visits and recurring visits often take the same drive and nearly the same time. Check the recurring price on its own, not only the first visit.
  • A margin on paper is not a margin per hour. Profit per tech hour shows which services are worth growing.
