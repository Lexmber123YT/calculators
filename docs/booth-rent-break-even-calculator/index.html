How this works

Your fixed costs per week are the rent plus your other monthly costs divided by 4.33 (the weeks in an average month). Each service leaves the price, minus its product cost, minus the card fee. Break-even is the fixed costs divided by what one service leaves, rounded up to a whole service.

For your goal, the page adds the monthly take-home you want, grossed up so the tax set-aside is covered, then divides by what one service leaves. The chair hours are that service count times the minutes per service. The last line is what one hour with a client leaves after product and fees, minus the rent and costs spread over your chair hours. Empty chair hours pay nothing, so your real hourly pay is lower until the chair is full.

Held at the kit's defaults: card fees are counted as if every client pays by card (a safe guess), and tips and retail sales are left out.

What the usual free answers miss

  • Rent divided by price ignores product, card fees and your other monthly bills. They are due whether you are booked or not.
  • Most stop at break-even. Break-even is the floor, where you work for free. The goal number is the one to plan around.
  • Few set tax aside before your take-home, so the goal comes out too low.
  • A client count means little if it does not fit your chair hours. The hours line tells you if it does.
  • A higher average price cuts the clients you need faster than a lower rent does. Try a $10 raise in the price box and compare.
