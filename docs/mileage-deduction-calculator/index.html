How this works

Each half of the year gets its own rate. Miles from January 1 to June 30 are counted at the first rate, and miles from July 1 on at the second. The boxes start at the IRS business rates for 2026: 72.5 cents a mile for the first half and 76 cents from July 1. You can type over them. Rates change, so check irs.gov for your year.

Then it takes the deduction off your profit for that half, and sets aside your percent of what is left. A half with a loss sets aside $0 and does not lower the other half. The last line is how much less the set-aside comes to because you logged the miles.

This is the kit's own math. The kit does it trip by trip and month by month, so each trip uses the rate in force on its date. This page lumps each half of the year together. That is close, but the kit is the record.

What the usual free answers miss

  • Many calculators use one rate for the whole year. In 2026 that is wrong for every mile on one side of July 1.
  • Only business miles count. Your normal commute and personal stops do not, and a dated log is what backs the number up.
  • The deduction is not cash back. It lowers the profit you owe tax on, so the real effect is roughly your set-aside percent of it.
  • Most stop at the deduction. Few show what it does to the money you move to savings for tax.
