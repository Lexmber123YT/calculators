How this works

First it works out the minutes. Bath and dry time comes from the size, the coat type and the condition. Haircut time comes from the size, the breed group, the service and the condition. Nail grinds add 10 minutes each and de-matting adds 15 minutes a block. Then 10 minutes go on for check-in, kennel and cleanup.

Then it costs those minutes. Groomer pay gets 10% added for payroll extras. Your monthly overhead is spread over your grooming hours, so a long groom carries more of the rent. Supplies go on top, by size and add-on.

Last, the price. The cost is divided by what is left of each dollar after your margin and a 3% card fee, then rounded up to the next $5. If you type the price you charge now, it checks that price: true cost, profit, margin and profit per groom hour. Held at the kit's usual numbers and not on the form: 10% payroll extras, 3% card fee, no commission, 10 extra minutes per groom, no van cost, and the kit's own time tables for each size, coat, breed group and service.

What the usual free answers miss

  • Most guides give a price range by weight. Coat and breed change the table time far more than a few pounds do, and time is what you pay for.
  • An overdue coat slows the bath and the haircut. Mats are their own time block, not a free extra.
  • Check-in, kennel time and cleanup are minutes you pay for. So is your share of rent for every hour the dog is in the shop.
  • The card fee comes off the top of every groom. A price that only covers your costs leaves no profit once it's taken.
