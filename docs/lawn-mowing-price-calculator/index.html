How this works

Mowing time is lawn size divided by how many square feet your mower covers in an hour, times a slowdown for obstacles and slope. If you have a crew, the trim, edge and blow work is split between them, but they all wait for the mower.

True cost adds up pay for everyone on site and in the truck (with 12% added for payroll taxes and insurance), fuel and equipment wear while on site, truck cost while driving, and one visit's share of your overhead. Price is cost divided by one minus your margin, rounded up to the next $5, and never under a $45 minimum.

To keep it short, a few things are held at the kit's starting values: fuel $4.50 and equipment $9 per hour on site, truck $14 per drive hour, 180 visits a month to share overhead across, no slowdown for gates, and weekly mowing. Mower speeds are rough, open-lawn numbers. Time a few of your own yards.

What the usual free answers miss

  • Price per hour or per square foot leaves out the drive. Small lawns cost more per square foot because you still drive, unload and pack up.
  • Overhead is rarely in the number. Insurance, phone and storage are paid whether you mow or not.
  • A margin is not a markup. A 30% margin means you keep 30% of the price, which is cost divided by 0.70.
  • Slopes and obstacles slow the mower, and most guides ignore them.
  • Local prices differ a lot. Use this as your floor, then look at what neighbors charge.
