How this works

For one stay: rent is nights times the nightly rate. The platform fee is charged on rent plus the cleaning fee. Profit per stay is rent plus cleaning fee, minus that fee, minus the turnover (cleaner, laundry, supplies). Profit per night is that divided by the nights.

For break-even, the page uses what one booked night keeps (the same as profit per night): the rate after the fee, plus the cleaning fee after its fee minus the turnover, spread over the stay. Your fixed costs for the year (monthly times 12) divided by what one night keeps gives the booked nights you need. Divide by 365 for the break-even occupancy. The cushion is your expected occupancy minus that, in percentage points.

Held at the kit's defaults: one booked stay length for the whole year, no seasons or weekend prices (use an average rate), and lodging tax left out, because guests pay it and it passes through to the tax office.

What the usual free answers miss

  • The quick rule, fixed costs divided by 365 times the rate, leaves out the fee and the turnovers, so it looks safer than it is. The page shows it next to the real number.
  • Many calculators take the fee on the rent only. A host-only fee is charged on the cleaning fee too.
  • Every stay carries one whole turnover. A 1-night stay can look fine by rate and still fall under your profit goal.
  • Fee models differ, so the fee is a box you can change, not a fixed number.
  • Break-even alone is a bare minimum. The cushion line shows how much room you have if a slow month hits.
