How much should I set aside for quarterly taxes when I'm self-employed?

Most quick answers take a percent of what customers pay you. Tax is owed on profit, after job costs, tools, overhead and miles. This works out a typical month's profit, sets your percent of it aside, and splits the year into the four estimated payments.

How this works

It takes your typical month and repeats it through 2026. Each month, profit is customer payments minus card fees, job costs, tools and gear, overhead, and your miles times the mileage rate for that month. Your tax pot percent of that profit is the amount to move to savings, rounded to the cent. A month with a loss moves $0 and does not lower the other months.

The months are then added up into the four IRS estimated payment periods. They are not four equal quarters: January to March, April and May, June to August, and September to December. The last one is due in January of the next year. Due dates move when they land on a weekend or holiday, so check irs.gov.

This is a set-aside estimate, not the IRS figure. It does not run tax brackets, credits, a W-2 job, your state, or last year's tax. Self-employment tax alone is roughly 14% of profit, which is why 25 to 30% is a common rule of thumb. Your preparer, or the worksheet that comes with the IRS estimated tax form, gives the real number.

What the usual free answers miss

  • A percent of what customers pay is too much in a heavy-materials month and too little in a light one. Tax follows profit.
  • Most split the year into four equal parts. The IRS periods are three, two, three and four months long.
  • Miles are a real cost on the return, and the 2026 rate went up on July 1. Many tools leave them out.
  • A loss month should set aside nothing. Averaging it in hides the months you do owe on.
