How this works

First it counts your real time. The minutes on the job, plus the admin minutes, plus the drive: round-trip miles turned into minutes at your average speed.

Then it takes out what the visit costs you: supplies plus miles times your gas and wear per mile. What is left of the price, spread over the real time, is your pay per hour now.

The price that reaches your goal is your hourly goal times the real time, plus the gas and supplies, rounded up to the next 50 cents. The raise you type is added to today's price and rounded to the nearest 50 cents, and the page shows what the new price pays per hour. Held at the kit's usual number and not on the form: prices round to $0.50.

What the usual free answers miss

  • Most pages quote a town average for a 30-minute walk. That tells you what others charge, not what the walk pays you.
  • The drive between clients is work time. So are the update texts and the photos. Leave them out and a $26 walk can look like $52 an hour when it pays far less.
  • Gas and bags are small per visit, but they come off every single one.
  • A raise is easier to judge when you see the pay per hour before and after. A few dollars can be the gap between below your goal and meeting it.
