How this works

Each draw is the contract price times its percent, rounded to the cent. When the percents add to 100%, the last draw is whatever is left after the others, so the draws always add up to the exact price (no stray penny). If they don't add to 100%, each draw is just its own percent and the check tells you how far off you are.

If you type a deposit cap, draw 1 is cut to the cap and the money it can't hold moves to draw 2. The total stays the same. Percents for draws past the number you picked are ignored.

Common draw schedules (rough starting points)

  • 2 draws: 50% at signing, 50% when the job is finished and signed off. Small jobs.
  • 3 draws: 40% / 50% / 10%. Signing, about half done, punch list signed off.
  • 4 draws: 30% / 30% / 30% / 10%. Signing, rough-in passes inspection, work finished, punch list.
  • 5 draws: 20% / 25% / 25% / 20% / 10%. Signing, materials on site, rough-in, finished, punch list.

What the usual free answers miss

  • Most lists give percents and stop. They don't check that your own percents add to 100%, or round so the dollars match the contract to the cent.
  • Some states cap the deposit on home work. A cap changes draw 2 too; few calculators show that.
  • Bank construction-loan draws are a different system. This is the payment plan between you and the homeowner.
  • A big last draw is the hardest to collect. Many contractors keep the final to about 5-10% so one small punch-list item doesn't hold up a large payment.
