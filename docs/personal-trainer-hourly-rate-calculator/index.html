What is my real hourly rate as a personal trainer?

A $75 session does not pay $75 an hour. Card fees, the gym's cut, the drive, setup, programming and messages all come out of it, and so do your yearly costs. This adds up a typical week and shows what each hour of work really pays.

How this works

It adds up one typical week. Clients pay you the session price (plus a small travel fee for a home or park visit past 10 miles). Then it takes off the card fee on each payment, the gym's fee or share for sessions at a gym, and the car cost of the drive. Your yearly costs are spread over 48 working weeks. What is left is what you keep before income tax.

Then it counts every hour: the sessions, setup minutes, the drive there and back, and your unpaid hours. Kept money divided by those hours is your real pay per hour. If you are under your target, it shows how much higher prices would need to be for this same week to reach it.

If your gym charges rent, the rent is counted for all 12 months a year and spread across your weeks. Held at the kit's usual numbers and not on the form: 48 weeks, a 30-cent flat card fee, 70 cents a mile for the car, 10 free miles then 60 cents a mile for travel, and setup and drive minutes for each place.

What the usual free answers miss

  • Many calculators divide income by billed hours. The real hours include programming, check-ins, messages and the drive.
  • The gym's cut is often mentioned and then left out of the number. Rent, a floor fee and a split each change the answer.
  • Card fees, car cost and yearly costs like insurance and certification renewals are small one at a time. Together they take a real bite from every session.
  • A rate means more with a goal beside it. The last line shows the price rise that would reach your target.
