How this works

It adds up one month. What you billed is your rate times your lesson hours. Take off the platform fees and you have what you kept. Take off your tutoring costs and your miles at the mileage rate, and you have profit before income tax. Divide that by every hour you worked: lessons, prep, and driving and admin. That is your real pay per hour.

For the goal, it works backward. Your goal times all the hours you work, plus your costs and mileage, is what the month has to bring in. Divide by your lesson hours and you get what you need to keep per lesson hour. The rate to charge adds back your platform fees spread over the lesson hours, so it assumes your fees stay about the same in dollars. If you don't use a platform, the two are the same.

The tax pot is your percent of the month's profit, and the last line is what is left per hour after it. This is the kit's Real Hourly Pay math for one month. The kit runs it on your real payments, costs and trips all year.

What the usual free answers miss

  • Most guides quote a rough range, often $25 to $80 an hour. That is what families pay, not what you keep.
  • Goal-based calculators assume every hour is billed. Prep, driving and messages can add a lot of unpaid time to each lesson.
  • Platform fees, workbooks, software and gas come off before you get paid. Few rate guides count them.
  • They rarely show today's real pay first. Knowing that number tells you whether you need a raise or fewer unpaid hours.
