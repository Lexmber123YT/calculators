How much should I charge for a small handyman job?

Most small jobs lose money because they are priced by the hour on site. The drive, the setup, the supply run and the truck and insurance bills are not on the clock. This works the price backward from the pay you want.

How this works

First it finds your hourly rate. Your take-home pay is grossed up for a 25% tax set-aside, your yearly business costs are added, and the total is spread over the job hours you work (48 weeks a year). That is the rate you need, rounded up to the next $5.

Then it prices the visit. Labor is your on-site hours plus 20 minutes of setup and cleanup, at that rate. On top go the trip charge, your materials at cost, your markup on them, and $35 for each supply run. If the total is under your job minimum, the minimum wins. The price is rounded up to the next $5.

Last, a profit check. It counts every hour the visit takes: on site, setup, the drive, and 30 minutes for each supply run. Then it divides what is left after materials by those hours. Held at the kit's usual numbers and not on the form: 48 weeks, 25% tax set-aside, 20 minutes setup, 30 minutes and $35 per supply run, and no dump fees.

What the usual free answers miss

  • Most guides quote a rough hourly range and stop. Your own rate depends on the pay you want and what your truck, tools and insurance cost, not on a town average.
  • Setup, cleanup, the drive and supply runs are hours you work. Pricing only the hours on site is why a quick fix can pay half what you thought.
  • Trip charges, markup and minimums are often shown as separate tips. Here they sit in one price, and the minimum is checked last, so a small job never goes out under it.
  • A price can be fine and still miss your goal. The last lines show what the visit pays per real hour so you can see it.
