How much should I charge for a vacation rental cleaning fee?

Most hosts copy the fee from the listings next door. But the booking platform often takes its cut from the cleaning fee too, and the cleaner is only part of the turn. This shows what you keep, what a turn really costs, and a fee that covers it.

How this works

First, what you keep: your cleaning fee minus the booking platform's cut. Then the turn cost: cleaner pay, plus laundry, plus supplies. The gap is what you keep minus the turn cost. A negative gap means every stay pays part of the clean out of your nightly rate.

The suggested fee takes cleaner pay plus laundry, adds 10% for the small extras (a re-clean, a stain, a lost key), adds supplies, then grosses it up for the platform's cut so you keep that much. It is rounded up to the next $5. The aim is to cover the turn, not to profit from it.

Held out of this page: the extra pay many hosts give a cleaner for a same-day turn. If you pay one, add it to cleaner pay for those turns.

What the usual free answers miss

  • Average fees by home size are old and local. Your own cleaner, laundry and supply costs are the real starting point.
  • On many fee plans the platform's cut comes out of the cleaning fee too. A $175 fee at 15.5% leaves you about $148.
  • Laundry and supplies are part of every turn, not part of "cleaning". Leave them out and a fee that looks fine runs short.
  • Guests see the cleaning fee next to the nightly rate. Check yours against nearby listings too, then decide where the gap goes.
