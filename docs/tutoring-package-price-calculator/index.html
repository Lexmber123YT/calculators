How this works

One lesson takes more than the lesson. Add the minutes for prep, travel and messages to get the hours it really takes. From the price, take off card or platform fees, travel cost and a share of your monthly business costs. Then take off your tax set-aside. What is left, divided by the real hours, is what you earn for an hour of work.

The goal price works backward from the take-home you want per hour. The package price is your single price times the lessons, less the discount, rounded to the nearest $5. The page then runs the same pay-per-hour check on the per-lesson price inside the package.

Two things are held at example values: monthly business costs of $90 spread over 26 lessons a month, and package prices rounded to the nearest $5. The kit lets you change both.

What the usual free answers miss

  • They show the family’s savings, not your pay. A 10% discount is $6 off a $60 lesson, but it comes out of the money left after costs, so it cuts your pay by more than 10%.
  • They count the lesson hour and nothing else. Prep, drive time and parent messages are real hours you do not bill.
  • They skip fees and tax. A platform that keeps a large cut changes the answer a lot; so does a tax set-aside.
  • They never check the pack against a goal. The per-lesson price falls with each bigger pack, so the longest pack usually pays the least per hour.
