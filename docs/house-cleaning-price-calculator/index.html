How this works

First, time. The page adds 60 minutes for every 1,000 square feet, 10 minutes per bedroom and 25 minutes per bathroom. Then it adjusts for condition (light 0.85, average 1.0, heavy 1.35), the type of clean (deep takes 1.8 times as long, a move-out 2.2 times) and how often the home is booked (weekly homes go faster, one-time cleans run long).

Second, cost. Labor is the crew hours times your hourly pay, plus 12% for payroll extras. Add supplies, the drive at 70 cents a mile, and your monthly overhead shared over 12 visits a month.

Third, price. The cost is divided by one minus your margin, then rounded up to the next $5. At a 25% margin, a visit that costs $100 is priced at $135, not $125. These are the same steps and defaults the kit uses. The extras, car cost and visits per month are held at the kit's defaults. The time rates are rough starting points, so time a few of your own jobs.

What the usual free answers miss

  • Rough rates of $0.05 to $0.15 per square foot ignore the home's condition and your own costs.
  • Hourly quotes forget payroll extras, the drive and overhead.
  • Margin is not markup. Adding 25% to your cost leaves you with only 20% of the price.
  • Most calculators stop at a price. This one also shows your profit per crew hour, so you can tell a good client from a slow one.
